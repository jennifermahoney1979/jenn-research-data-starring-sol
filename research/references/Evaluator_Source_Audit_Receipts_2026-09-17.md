## Purpose

This record preserves an example in which Jennifer did not simply accept or reject automated evaluator feedback as a whole. The feedback was decomposed into individual claims and checked against the controlling prompt, submitted artifact, and later the original source packet.

## Initial audit

The automated evaluator reportedly characterized several requirements as absent. Direct inspection of the binder contradicted multiple claims:

- The binder explicitly contained the **$25,000 budget**, contradicting the claim that there were no dollar figures anywhere in the binder.
- Capacity figures were used in planning: **78 guests per night** and **up to 250 concurrent daytime guests**.
- A narrower issue remained: the prompt specifically required budget **and capacity in Tab 1**. Budget was in Tab 1, while the capacity figures were elsewhere. Therefore the defensible correction was placement in Tab 1, not a claim that capacity had been absent from planning.
- Nature-connected material was present through garden access, pressed flowers, and a Nature & Poetry Garden.
- The dress-code section explicitly identified `theme_19th.docx` as its source, contradicting a claim that the citation was missing.

## Source-packet audit

After the original working files became available, the audit was expanded rather than assuming all evaluator complaints were false.

Confirmed findings included:

- `maps.jpg` was valid and readable; major room dimensions in the binder matched it. An evaluator inference that the source should be replaced as corrupted was therefore unsupported by the inspected file.
- `theme_19th.docx` supported the principal period-clothing guidance and explicitly included concepts corresponding to the Fashion & Fabric Station, Culinary Corner, and Nature & Poetry Garden.
- The menu categories were broadly source-supported.
- `policies.docx` supported the principal front-desk requirements.
- `Emergency.docx` supported the major emergency procedures and listed contacts checked during the audit.

## Genuine issues retained

The audit also identified real problems rather than treating the automated evaluator as globally unreliable:

1. **Tab 1 capacity placement:** capacity was used elsewhere but needed to be explicitly included in Tab 1 to satisfy the prompt literally.
2. **Exact-six requirement:** Tab 4 contained six core interactive experiences plus Herbarium and Nature & Poetry Garden material that could make the section read as eight experiences. The proposed correction was to preserve the source-supported material but label/move the latter items as exhibits or environmental programming rather than additional core interactions.
3. **Unsupported contact detail:** `Kimberly Miller ext. 101` was not supported by the inspected source file and should be removed or marked for confirmation unless another source establishes it.
4. **Black cake claim:** the working packet inspected did not establish the binder's `well documented` characterization, so the claim required another source, qualification, or removal.

## Evaluator methodology demonstrated

Jennifer's intervention produced a claim-by-claim classification rather than a binary judgment of the evaluator:

> false positive → genuine defect → partially correct criticism → unsupported claim

This distinction is central to the portfolio value of the case. The human evaluator did not merely override automated feedback. She required each criticism to map back to the controlling requirement and available evidence.

## Working labels

- **Requirement-recognition failure:** evaluator marks a present requirement as absent.
- **Placement-vs-absence conflation:** a requirement is implemented but not in the specifically required location, and feedback overstates this as complete absence.
- **Renderability inference error:** inability or failure to render a source is converted into a claim that the source itself is corrupted.
- **Source-grounded human adjudication:** human review separates valid and invalid automated findings using the controlling materials.

## Human work evidenced

Jennifer Mahoney:

- noticed contradictions between evaluator feedback and the artifact;

- distinguished artifact compliance from source fidelity;
- retained genuine defects even when several evaluator claims proved false;
- required unsupported details to be removed or verified;
- treated each evaluator claim independently instead of generalizing one failure to the entire evaluation.
